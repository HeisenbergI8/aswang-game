# Research 03 — bahay kubo interiors, storage, windows, the sari-sari store

Sourcing: **✅ sourced** · **⚠️ snippet-only** (search index of a page that 403'd) · **❌ unsourced**.

Seven interiors are about to be built and players will walk into them to search. This is the prop list.

---

## 1. Layout

✅ The raised living area is **"a single multi-purpose open room"** — living, dining, cooking and
sleeping at once. — [Wikipedia — Bahay kubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahay_kubo)

⚠️ **Larger** houses partition: *"Sawali partition divides the **bulwagan** and **silid**, where chests
and woven trunks (**tampipi**) keep clothes."* And privacy in a one-room house is made with cloth: *"a
thin cloth curtain strung over an opening stakes out a private section."* — SEAsite NIU (TLS cert
mismatch; snippet-only)

**Build implication.** Seven identical open rooms is both inaccurate and bad for a hidden-monster game.
**Five open-plan, two with a sawali half-wall or a hanging curtain** is sourced *and* gives sightline
variety — and a curtain is one part.

⚠️ Floor area **30–51 m² (320–550 sq ft)**; stilts **1–2 m**. — Grokipedia (403) corroborated by the
SEAsite summary. ❌ **Ceiling height and roof pitch: no source at all.** Wikipedia explicitly gives no
dimensions. Use ~2.1–2.4 m at the wall plate under a steep roof, as convention.

**Reality check that matters** ⚠️ 2020 census: only **11.1%** of dwellings have bamboo/sawali/cogon/nipa
walls (60.4% concrete/brick/stone), and **85.9% have galvanized-iron roofs** against 4.7% cogon/nipa.
32.2% of households cook with wood. — [PSA 2020 CPH](https://psa.gov.ph/content/housing-characteristics-philippines-2020-census-population-and-housing) (403; snippet-only)

**So a present-day barrio reads as rusted GI roofing over sawali or half-concrete walls.** The map is
all-nipa. That is a defensible remote-barrio choice but it should be a **deliberate** one — raised in
the plan's Follow Ups rather than changed, since the realism plan tuned the nipa palette.

## 2. The five details that do the most work

Each is ✅ sourced and each is unmistakable.

**1. Banggera / banggerahan — the best single object available.** ✅ A counter of **slatted wood that
protrudes outward** from the kitchen toward the back lawn, for washing and air-drying. *"Cups are
typically **inverted on wooden sticks**, while plates are placed **between or above the slats**."* A
**tapayan** water jar commonly sits at either end. — [Wikipedia — Banggéra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangg%C3%A9ra),
[ManilaNews](https://manilanews.ph/bahay-kubo/) — **and it reads from OUTSIDE the house**, which is
worth more than any interior prop.

**2. The tukod window.** ✅ *"The most traditional are **large awning windows, held open by a wooden
rod**."* ⚠️ **Tukod** is the rod's name — the windows *"have tukod or 'legs' that hold the swinging
shades open during the day, and secure it back in place at night."* — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahay_kubo);
[Jennifer Hallock](https://www.jenniferhallock.com/2016/07/01/bahaykubo/) (cert error, snippet-only)

A hinged sawali panel swung outward and up on a stick: the opening reads as a **dark slot under a
shading flap**, and at night the panel drops shut. For a horror game the stick is a physical affordance.

**3. The altar.** ✅ Family altars hold religious figurines, blessed candles, rosaries — commonly a
**Santo Niño**, the Virgin, patron saints — alongside **photographs of deceased relatives**, a crucifix
and fresh sampaguita. *"What is commonly seen in Filipino living rooms is a Santo Niño statuette."* —
[Catholically](https://www.catholically.com/blogs/news/how-to-set-up-a-catholic-home-altar-a-step-by-step-guide),
[Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/entertainment/2006/04/11/330945/religious-icons-our-home)

**Framed photos of the dead standing beside the saints** is the specific sourced touch — and it is
perfect horror furniture for an aswang game. Candles must be `Neon`, never a `PointLight`.

**4. The hanging sachet strips** at the sari-sari window — see §5.

**5. The single shared glass** at the inuman — see §5.

## 3. Storage — the gameplay-critical list

Six shapes, no two confusable at distance, which is the property a search mechanic needs.

**Tampipi** ⚠️ *"chests and **woven trunks (tampipi)** keep clothes and personal belongings"* (SEAsite),
✅ corroborated independently as *"**tampipi** for storing clothing and blankets"* —
[Milwaukee Public Museum](https://www.mpm.edu/index.php/research-collections/anthropology/online-collections-research/philippine-basketry-collection-2).
**A lidded woven box that lifts open — and it is the right one for a poor hut.**

**Banga / tapayan** ✅ **30 cm to over 100 cm**, *"bulbous shoulders with flat bases"*, up to eight
handles, early native ones **mostly unglazed**. Water jars are *"normally kept in the banggera"*. —
[Wikipedia — Tapayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapayan) — **the best cited dimensions in this
whole research pass; use the range directly.**

**Baul / aparador** ✅ Carved hardwood chest and wardrobe for *"clothes, money, jewelry and important
documents"* and *"linens and heirlooms"*. — [Federal Land](https://federalland.ph/articles/different-types-of-traditional-filipino-furniture/)
⚠️ **Class caveat:** these are *bahay na bato* objects, not a poor farmer's. Use for the one better-off
hut — which is a free way to differentiate a house.

**Platera / pamingganan** ✅ the sideboard dishes go into *after* drying on the banggera. —
[ManilaNews](https://manilanews.ph/bahay-kubo/)

**Sako** ✅ *"a large container for rice, palay, or other grains"*; retail sizes 25 kg and 50 kg, small
ones **35 × 50 cm**. — [TagalogLang](https://www.tagaloglang.com/sako/)

**Silong** ✅ stores *"harvested crops, tools, and other implements"*, houses *"chickens, pigs, or
goats"*, and holds *"mortars and pestles and other heavy items"* — including the **lusong**, a
waist-high mortar hollowed from a tree trunk. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahay_kubo),
[ManilaNews](https://manilanews.ph/bahay-kubo/)

⚠️ **Rafter storage is sourced only for the Cordillera** (a Kankanay attic granary above the hearth),
not for a lowland kubo. ❌ Blue plastic drums, repurposed biscuit tins, plastic bins, a bolo hung on a
nail: all ubiquitous, none citable.

## 4. Kitchen and household objects

✅ All from [VeraFiles — *No plastics: Recalling the early Filipino
kitchen*](https://verafiles.org/articles/no-plastics-recalling-the-early-filipino-kitchen): **banga**
(clay water jar), **bilao** (round flat winnowing basket), **palayok/kulon** (round-bottomed clay
pots), **kalan/pugon** (earthenware charcoal stove), **kawa** (large vat), **kawali**, **kudkuran**
(coconut grater with serrated blade), **lusong** (trunk mortar), **dikdikan** (granite mortar),
**sandok** (coconut-shell ladle).

✅ **Abuhan** — *"an elevated area of the floor packed with soil… containing the fireplace with clay or
stone trivets."* **The fire sits on packed earth inside a bamboo house.** ✅ **Dirty kitchen** — an
outdoor or annexed kitchen, separated for fire safety, smoke and grime; *"**palayok, kalan,
panggatong** are prominent fixtures."* — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahay_kubo),
[Wikipedia — Dirty kitchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirty_kitchen),
[OhOpen](https://ohopen.com/native-dirty-kitchen-design-philippines/)

✅ **Batalan** — a projecting rear wet area with **looser floorboards to drain faster**, holding large
water jars. ✅ **Tabo in a timba** — a dipper kept **floating inside** a pail, *"found at the entrance
of the house, next to a terracotta water jar so guests can wash their hands and feet before
entering."* — [Wikipedia — Tabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabo_(hygiene))
**Two objects by the door, zero cost, unmistakably right.**

✅ **Gasera** — the kerosene lamp, *"the primary light source in rural Philippine homes before
electrification,"* still **"the last resort" during brownouts**; dim. One auctioned specimen measured
**16.5 cm tall, 7 cm base**. — [TagalogNow](https://www.tagalognow.com/dictionary/gasera/),
[Moreton Auctions](https://bid.moretonauctions.com/lots/view/5-1NT9W5/philippines-gasera-lamp-l-165-cm-bottom-width-7-cm)
**Small enough to be a hand prop, dim enough to justify a horror light radius.**

✅ **Monobloc chairs** and a **borrowed electric fan** — both attested in the inuman description below.
✅ **Bayong** — woven market bag, buri in the Visayas, pandan in Luzon. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayong)

❌ Wall calendar from a store or politician; family photo collage (photos *on the altar* are ✅);
rosary hung on a nail; indoor clothesline; sacks of rice in the kitchen corner; a TV. **The 2022 NDHS
has rural appliance-ownership rates split urban/rural across 30,372 households — all three PDF
endpoints returned 403. The number exists; it is not in hand.**

## 5. Sari-sari store and inuman

**The store is part of a house** ✅ *"family-run and privately owned, **operating within the shopkeeper's
residence**"*; **~93% are in residential communities**; *"a small stall or room in someone's house
**with a grille window through which items are sold**."* Goods are displayed in *"a large
screen-covered or metal-barred window"*; candies in recycled jars; cooking oil, salt and sugar in back
storage. — [Wikipedia — Sari-sari store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sari-sari_store)

✅ Typical fit-out: grills kept **wide open during the day** and locked at night; **three metal shelves
with five levels each**; best-sellers at eye level; a **chest freezer**; a bright **tarpaulin** sign;
**a couple of plastic chairs or a bench and a small table** out front. —
[Trailblazer](https://trailblazer.com.ph/how-to-design-a-sari-sari-store/)

✅ **The hanging sachets** — *"hanging sachets of coffee, shampoo, and snacks dancing in the wind"*,
plywood shelves, a counter. The mechanism is **hanging strips and a puncher**: a hole punched in each
item's top and hung in a slot; also pegboards and hanging baskets. —
[Discover Philippines](https://www.discoverphilippines.org/p/the-filipino-sari-sari-store),
[Truehost](https://truehost.ph/modern-sari-sari-store-design-ideas/)

✅ **Tingi** — buying single units rather than packages; Filipinos consume roughly **591 sachets per
person per year**. — [Wikipedia — Tingi culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tingi_culture)

**The sachet strips are the money shot for this game specifically:** they occlude sightlines, they move,
and nothing else on Roblox has them.

**The store IS the drinking shed** ✅ *"The benches provided in front of the store are usually occupied
by local people; some men spend time **drinking** there while women discuss the latest local news."*
— [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sari-sari_store). **No separate structure is needed**, and
❌ the standalone drinking shed as a building type is unsourced.

**Inuman** ✅ Held *"in a **local sari sari store**, a roadside restaurant, a karaoke bar, or the living
room of somebody using **plastic chairs and a borrowed electric fan**."* ✅ **One glass is shared**: a
designated **taga-tagay** / **tanggero** fills it and passes it to each person in turn, who drinks and
hands it back — *"everyone drinks the same, from the same glass."* Refusing a turn is prohibited
without a valid reason. ✅ **Pulutan** is required; a session without food is *"somehow disrespectful."*
— [Humaling](https://humaling.com/tagay-a-look-at-philippine-drinking-culture/),
[Wikipedia — Drinking culture of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drinking_culture_of_the_Philippines)

**So the inuman prop cluster is ONE glass and ONE bottle at the centre of a seated ring**, a plate of
pulutan, monoblocs, a fan on an extension cord. **Five glasses would be the tell that no Filipino was
in the room.**

⚠️ **"Alay sa Demonyo"** — pouring the first splash on the ground as an offering — is documented as a
pre-drinking ritual. — Grokipedia (snippet-only). **A shed where someone poured out an offering to the
demonyo before dark is free thematic gold for this game**, and it is one decal.

## 6. Windows — and one direct answer

**Capiz is NOT right for a rural kubo, and this is sourced rather than taste.** ✅ Capiz windows were
used *"extensively"* in **bahay na bato**, at least a metre high, with a broad grooved sill carrying
two sets of sliding shutters and a **ventanilla** below. ✅ Explicitly: *"Houses with capiz windows and
grand staircases reflect the **wealth and status** of families… characteristic of affluent bahay na bato
residences **rather than simpler structures like nipa huts**."* — [Renacimiento
Manila](https://renacimientomanila.org/2020/06/21/trivia-no-10-the-multi-purpose-functionality-and-sustainability-of-bahay-na-bato/),
[UnknownCebu](https://unknowncebu.com/2024/11/28/capiz-windows-in-the-philippines-history-and-examples/)

**So: default all seven to tukod-propped sawali awnings. Give capiz sliding panes and a ventanilla to
exactly one** — the captain's or the store owner's house — and it reads instantly as "this family has
money." A free storytelling lever, and it is the plan's "rich" interior tier.

✅ **Hagdan** — *"a **bamboo or wooden ladder** that extends from the ground to the door or to a small
open porch"* (**balkon/beranda**). With 1–2 m stilts that is **4–7 rungs**: a chokepoint one player at
a time, which is a real gameplay property. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahay_kubo)

❌ **Front door dimensions, and whether it is a leaf or a curtain: no source.** Interior curtains are
⚠️ sourced; extending that to the entrance is inference.

**Note the security implication, which is architecture rather than design compromise:** windows are
large, propped open by day, and the walls are woven bamboo. **Nothing here keeps an aswang out**, and
the tukod stick is the only thing between open and closed.

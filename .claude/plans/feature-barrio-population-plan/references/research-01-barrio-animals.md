# Research 01 — the six forms: barrio animals and villagers

Sourcing convention, kept from the realism plan: **✅ sourced** (URL given) · **⚠️ snippet-only** (came
from a search index of a page that returned 403 to a direct fetch) · **❌ unsourced** (convention, no
citation found, built anyway and flagged).

**Why this document exists.** Six mesh prompts and eighteen spawn coordinates are about to be written.
The default failure is Western: a Labrador, a pink Landrace pig, a Nubian goat, a Leghorn hen, and a
villager in a poncho. Every correction below is cheap at prompt time and expensive afterwards.

---

## 1. DOG — *aspin* / *askal*

**Name** ✅ *Aspin* contracts **asong Pinoy**; coined by PAWS in 2007 to replace **askal** (*asong
kalye*, street dog). Cebuano: *irong Bisaya*. — [Wikipedia — Askal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Askal)

**Appearance** ✅ Medium, **no more than two feet at the shoulder**, roughly 12–19 in. Coat **short and
rough**. Bone structure **"on the medium range, never heavy"**. **Tail usually held high.** Ears
floppy, semi-floppy *or* fully erect — **mixed within one population**, so a barrio should show both.
**Spots commonly at the base of the tail and on the back in semi-circular fashion** — the single most
distinctive marking. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Askal),
[Philippine Primer](https://primer.com.ph/tips-guides/2017/09/03/expats-guide-to-aspins/)

**Where** ✅ Free-ranging but mostly **owned**: a Bulacan household survey found **54.1% of households
owned dogs**, 1.21 per household, **31% roaming freely**, dog-to-human ratio 1:3.7. They are "allowed
out to roam the neighbourhood but **expected to be home before dusk**." — [PLOS
NTD](https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pntd.0009948),
[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Askal)

**Territory implication.** Home-by-dusk roaming is why DOG's territory is the **roads** — the one
thoroughfare form, and therefore the most permissive disguise in the game. That spread against PIG's is
deliberate.

❌ Lying in road dust / doorway shade / under parked tricycles: convention.

## 2. CAT — *puspin*

**Name** ✅ *Pusang Pinoy*; generic *pusa*. — [Pusa.PH](https://pusa.ph/blogs/the-meow-nila-bulletin-pusa-phs-official-blog/types-of-pusa-meet-the-many-faces-of-the-proud-puspin)

**Appearance** ⚠️ Short coats. **"Puspin's tail might be regular length or it can be shortened,
'stumpy,' or kinked. This is widespread in many Domestic Short Haired cats in Asia"** — genetically
related to the Japanese Bobtail. — Manila Standard / Our Brew, snippet-only (both 403).

✅ Locally-named colour types: **orange** (mostly male), **"tilapia"** (mottled, said to resemble fish
scales), calico, tortoiseshell, solid black, white with soft tabby markings. — [Pusa.PH](https://pusa.ph/blogs/the-meow-nila-bulletin-pusa-phs-official-blog/types-of-pusa-meet-the-many-faces-of-the-proud-puspin)

**Where** ✅ **"crowded streets, dark alleys, busy markets, and in the corners of neighborhoods"**;
market cats **return to the same spots** and keep rats down. — [CARA Welfare
Philippines](https://www.caraphil.org/their-lives-matter-too-the-unseen-reality-of-stray-cats-amp-dogs-in-the-philippines/)

**Territory implication.** "Dark alleys" is the direct source for CAT's three alley territories, and
"return to the same spots" is the behavioural justification for the leash.

**The kinked/stumpy tail is the highest-value single detail in this document** — it reads as not-Western
for one bone. ❌ Body build and coat length beyond "short" are convention.

## 3. PIG — the native backyard *baboy*

⚠️ **Do not confuse with *baboy damo / ramo***, the wild Philippine warty pig — a different animal.

**Appearance** ⚠️ **"Small and late maturing, mostly solid black and white with small ears, sway back
and with weak pasterns."** Coarse thick black coat; **elongated hairs form a crest down the centre of
the back**; **tuft of long black hairs at the tail tip**. ~60 kg over 6–8 months. — The Pig Site
(403, snippet-only). ✅ Cordillera native black pig: **black, ~85 kg, small ears**. — [Slow Food Ark of
Taste](https://www.fondazioneslowfood.com/en/ark-of-taste-slow-food/cordillera-native-black-pig/)

**The silhouette is: sway back + belly near the ground + small ears + dorsal bristle crest + tail
tassel.** Closer to a pot-bellied pig than to a pink Landrace.

**How kept — the answer that decides the territory** ✅ *"Native pigs can be seen **tied under shady
trees or roaming freely around backyards**."* The source frames this as the practice extension officers
are trying to *replace* with pens — i.e. it is what you actually see. — [Agriculture Monthly](https://agriculture.com.ph/2018/10/25/native-pig-raising/)

✅ When penned: *kulungan* **2 × 2 m** per animal, roof of nipa/cogon/anahaw, **walls of bamboo and
coconut husk**, deep bedding of coir dust, sawdust or rice hull. That is the prop palette. — same source

✅ Also kept in the **silong**, the shaded space under a raised house. — [Kollective
Hustle](https://www.kollectivehustle.com/blog/the-humble-bahay-kubo)

❌ **Wallowing in mud has no Philippine citation.** It is universal pig thermoregulation and safe to
build, but it is the visual the whole PIG territory rests on and it is convention, not evidence.

## 4. GOAT — *kambing*

**Appearance** ✅ **Small, stocky, low-set.** Black-brown predominant; some reddish-brown, some solid
white. **Ears erect. Horns straight and erect.** Straight face profile, no wattles, plain hair.
**~20 kg, range 10.5–24.4 kg** — knee-to-mid-thigh on an adult. — [Bondoc & Garcia,
UPLB](https://www.ukdr.uplb.edu.ph/journal-articles/3155/), [Oriental Mindoro phenotypic
characterization](https://ejournals.ph/article.php?id=15465)

**The erect ear is the tell.** An "upgraded" goat (native × Anglo-Nubian/Boer) is larger with long
pendulous ears — building that says crossbreed, not native.

**How kept — fully sourced, and it is the model for the whole territory system** ✅ **Tethering with
rotational grazing, 72.73% of raisers**; housing mainly bamboo slats; herd size 1–5. — [Camiguin
management study](https://innspub.net/management-practices-of-goats-capra-hircus-in-catarman-camiguin-philippines/)

✅ *"Goats are **tethered near or around vacant croplands**, and tethering **under coconuts and other
plantation tree crops** is common. Goats tethered during the day are **secured near or underneath the
farmer's house at night**."* — [Central Luzon smallhold study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/372469550_Farm_characteristics_management_practices_and_production_performance_of_smallholder_goat_farms_in_Central_Luzon)

**Territory implication.** A goat on a rope at a field edge is not a game constraint dressed as
realism — it is the documented default. **The rope and stake are the prop that makes the rule visible**
and Phase 8 owes them.

## 5. CHICKEN — *manok*

**Appearance** ✅ **Darag** (Panay): hen yellowish-brown, each feather dark brown and golden yellow with
**a distinct yellow line base-to-tip**; rooster red with black. Both: **single comb, whitish earlobe,
grey/slate shanks** — the slate shank is a named identifying feature. Hen **~1.0 kg**, cock ~1.3 kg. —
[PCAARRD-DOST](https://www.pcaarrd.dost.gov.ph/index.php/quick-information-dispatch-qid-articles/flavors-of-science-how-science-created-an-authentic-ilonggo-flavor-darag-native-chicken),
[Slow Food](https://www.fondazioneslowfood.com/en/ark-of-taste-slow-food/darag-chicken/)

✅ **Flocks are not uniform** — Banaba, Bolinao, Paraoakan (largest, long-legged, usually black),
Joloanon (tall, tricoloured) all coexist. — [Chicken Breed
Guide](https://chickenbreedguide.com/breeds/all-about-philippine-native-chicken-breeds/)

**Where** ✅ **"Most of the 80 million native Philippine chickens live in small groups as free-range
animals, pecking out a decent living in the backyards of rural homes."** Flocks up to ~24 hens. —
[The Poultry Site](https://www.thepoultrysite.com/articles/farming-heritage-chicken-breeds-of-the-philippines)

**Territory implication.** "Backyards of rural homes" is the direct source for siting CHICKEN beside
three kubo.

**Two behaviour findings worth building** ✅ Chickens spend **~61% of active time foraging** — pecking
and scratching — and do it even when fed. **Idle animation should be near-continuous ground-scratching,
not standing still.** — [Alabama Cooperative
Extension](https://www.aces.edu/blog/topics/farming/common-backyard-chicken-behaviors/)

✅ **Roosting in trees at night** is documented as instinct and as the widespread night-enclosure
system in tropical village flocks, driven by escape from ground predators. — [CABI Agriculture and
Bioscience](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43170-021-00040-z) — **silhouetted chickens up in
branches is a free, sourced, atmospheric beat for a night game.**

**Sabong** ✅ The A-frame shelter is called a **tee-pee** or "Indian house", *"an inverted V that cocks
can hide into"*, ideal **2 ft wide × 4 ft high**, of cement, bamboo, wood or plywood, with a perch. Tie
cords are standard kit. — [UNAHCO Gamefowl Breeding
101](https://unahco.com/news-events/gamefowl-breeding-101-facilities-equipment/) ❌ The *layout* — a
spaced grid of one leashed cock per hut — is unsourced. Note PH sources say **inverted-V**, not cone.

## 6. VILLAGER

**Clothing** ✅ **Sando** — sleeveless undershirt, *"a symbol of the working class,"* standard everyday
male wear at home. Plain white cotton. — [TagalogNow](https://www.tagalognow.com/dictionary/sando/)

✅ **Daster / duster** — *"the localized term for the duster… the ubiquitous, traditional house dress
worn mostly by mothers and grandmothers."* Loose, shin-length, **vibrant tropical prints** —
gumamela, sampaguita, palm fronds. — [Vogue PH](https://vogue.ph/fashion/shopping/these-summer-dresses-look-good-all-year-long/),
[Shop Cambio](https://www.shopcambio.co/blogs/news/an-ode-to-the-filipino-duster-embracing-the-beauty-of-my-lola-s-house-dress)

✅ **Tsinelas** — flip-flops, the default footwear. ✅ **Salakot** — dome- or cone-shaped, **crown
carrying a spiked or knobbed finial**, inner headband and chinstrap; bamboo, rattan, nito or gourd.
*"Most salakot were simple and made from cheap materials… worn as protection against the sun and rain
by farmers and fishermen."* — [Wikipedia — Salakot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salakot)

⚠️ **DO NOT USE A MALONG.** It is traditional to **Bangsamoro and mainland Mindanao / Sulu** groups,
**not** a Tagalog or Visayan lowland barrio. This is a real geographic error, not a nuance. —
[Wikipedia — Malong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malong). ❌ *Tapis*: no adequate citation found;
treat with the same caution.

**Where they are in the evening** ✅ This is the best-sourced item here and it settles VILLAGER's
territory: *"**The benches provided in front of the store are usually occupied by local people**; some
men spend time drinking there while women discuss the latest local news, youths also use the place to
hang out."* — [Wikipedia — Sari-sari store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sari-sari_store)

✅ **Tambay** — *"they're experts at finding a spot — usually in front of a sari-sari store, under a
shady tree, or near a **kanto** (street corner)."* — [Nipino](https://www.nipino.com/tambay-filipino-hanging-out-culture)

**So the drinking shed IS the sari-sari bench.** No separate structure is needed, and the standalone
shed as a building type is ❌ unsourced.

✅ **Dusk is chore time**, which is exactly this game's clock: tethered goats are brought in at night
and dogs are expected home before dusk.

❌ **Posture is entirely unsourced** — the squat, the towel over the shoulder, the arm hooked over a
knee. Only *sitting on benches in front of the store* and *standing at a corner or under a tree* are
cited.
